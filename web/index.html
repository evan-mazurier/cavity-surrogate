How to read the picture

  • The box. A square cavity full of fluid, seen from the side. Three walls are fixed; the top wall (the lid) slides to the right at speed 1 and drags the fluid with it.
  • Colour = speed. Dark blue is fluid nearly at rest, yellow is moving as fast as the lid. Fastest just under the lid, slowest in the eye of the vortex and in the corners.
  • Arrows = direction. The lid pushes fluid right, it turns down the right wall, back along the bottom, up the left: one big clockwise whirlpool.
  • White lines = streamlines, the paths fluid particles follow. Closed loops = recirculation. The + marks the eye of the main vortex.
  • Orange loops = corner eddies, small whirlpools in the bottom corners spinning the opposite way. They grow with Re; above Re ≈ 2000 a third one appears at the top left.

Re (Reynolds number) is the only knob: momentum versus viscosity. Low Re = honey, one lazy vortex sitting high. High Re = water, a stronger vortex that moves to the centre and bigger corner eddies.

Inside the training range the surrogate matches the solver to about one part in a million — the solver's own convergence floor. Outside it the error climbs at once: the model interpolates a validated solver, it does not contain the physics.

What the model is

86 runs of a Navier–Stokes solver (validated against Ghia et al. 1982, see the repo) were reduced to 20 spatial patterns (POD modes). Each pattern's weight is a smooth curve in log Re. Every picture here is mean + Σ aₖ(Re) · patternₖ, computed in this page from 2.7 MB of numbers.
